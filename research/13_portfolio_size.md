# Portfolio-size comparison

All sizes use the same momentum family, cost model, current-universe proxy, monthly selection and quarterly weight correction.

| Size | Pre-holdout active TWR vs QQQ | Holdout active TWR vs QQQ | Pre-holdout turnover | Holdout turnover | Interpretation |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 10 | +5.43 pp | +8.29 pp | 936.8% | 874.6% | Attractive but operationally extreme and highly concentrated |
| 20 | +4.72 pp | +5.74 pp | 265.5% | 258.1% | Positive but far above turnover limit |
| 30 | +1.72 pp | -0.83 pp | 184.0% | 172.0% | Frozen primary; failed holdout and turnover |
| 40 | +0.96 pp | -4.93 pp | 108.6% | 115.1% | Near turnover cap but unstable return |
| 50 | -0.01 pp | -12.37 pp | 26.7% | 37.9% | Operationally easier but failed return hurdle |

No tested size combined return against both benchmarks, acceptable turnover, holdout stability, concentration control and credible data integrity. Selecting 10 or 20 stocks because their observed return is higher would replace one failure with concentration and turnover risk, while exploiting the current-winner universe bias.

The result supports the preregistered principle that portfolio size cannot be selected on return alone.
